One correction before you lean on it: the document's forms of the settings are not settings-fields' shapes. jig's document decoder takes `waiting_ms`/`time_ms`/`call_timeout_ms` where the settings file takes nanoseconds named `waiting`/`time`/`call_timeout`; the document's authority has a `budget` object (`spend`, `deadline_unix_seconds`) where the file's has `spend` and `deadline` in nanoseconds; its pattern has `segments` as text plus `value`, the file's hex segments and a `last` shape; its policy has no `permissions` (the document maps permissions in `engine.projects[].roles`).

So, proposed: the four rows (`engine.charters`, `templates`, `policies`, `rules`) say what main does with them and send the reader to `settings show` and `settings apply` (`temper help settings`), with no claim about their inner form; and `engine.projects[]` (init writes project 1 with its policy) keeps rows of the document's own policy, role, authority, grant, pattern and requirement shapes, since nothing else documents that form. A survey (S4, running) says what a later start does with each member, so the rows say whether a change after the first start applies, is ignored or is refused. Say if you want otherwise.
