# temper's store

Provisional, 2026-10-05, revised 2026-10-23. What temper keeps in jig's
store, and how: its families, the forge's records, under the
application's tag; their keys, their records and temper's store
version; and the restart's ranges. The store itself is jig's
(jig's `protocol/store.md`): `jig-protocol-store`, skein's key-value
store in the engine's process, with transcripts as files and secrets in
a second store. temper's part is the crate `temper-protocol-store`
(`README.md`, section 5), engine only. What is open is listed in section
7.

## 1. In one page

- **jig's store, temper's families.** The engine's protocol layer owns
  jig's store component; temper adds its own records beside jig's,
  under the application's tag, in the same commits (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, section 12).
- **One family per forge record.** Each variant of the forge connector's
  `Stored` is a family, a keyed adoption's or forge outcome and each
  forge's writer among them. Nothing
  else of temper's is in the main store: temper has no index of its own
  and no secret record there, its forges' records being in jig's
  secrets' store (credentials.md, 2.1); the core's records, people's and
  tasks' are jig's.
- **Keys sort as the forge's keys do:** a family byte in the variants'
  order, then the key's fields by jig's key rule; a property test holds
  each encoder to the derived order (section 3).
- **Records from a schema,** generated by skein's generator, translated
  to and from the domain's by total functions; `STORE_VERSION` names
  their encodings (section 4).
- **All live.** The forge's restart step restores every family of
  temper's as one interval, the root's `Range::Forge`; nothing of
  temper's is loaded on demand (section 5).
- **The core's ranges are jig's, filtered.** The root's core ranges are
  asked as jig's coarser ones and filtered, and its census is jig's walk,
  taken as it comes (section 5).
- **Sized from limits.** The largest key and record follow from the
  forge's limits, and jig's component checks them with its own at start
  (section 4).
- **The real store in temper's worlds,** over skein-kv's in-memory mode,
  and on the simulator's crashing disk in the one-process world
  (section 6).
- **jig's, cited, not restated:**
  - transcripts: one file per task, written before their commit (jig's
    `protocol/store.md`, section 8);
  - secrets: a second store, holding temper's forges' records under
    temper's tag (credentials.md, 2.1) and the values clients' requests
    carry, kept by jig until their run ends (credentials.md, 2.2); both
    written and read by the engine's protocol layer, never by this
    crate (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 9);
  - opening: the format row and the header checked before the root
    starts (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 10);
  - fill: reported with each commit, new work refused above the
    high-water mark (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 11.2);
  - retention: the core's ended work shrunk past horizons (jig's
    `protocol/store.md`, 11.1); temper's families have none (section 7).

## 2. temper's families

One per variant of `temper_engine_domain_forge::Stored`
(`crates/temper-engine-domain-forge/src/boundary.rs`), in the order of
its variants, which is also the order of its `Key`. Each is restored
whole at the forge's restart step (section 5), and bounded by the
forge's limits (`temper_engine_domain_forge::Limits`, configuration.md,
section 7).

| 0 | `Call` | `Call(NamedCall)`: task, attempt, completion, position | the connector's part of a live named call | `named_calls` |
| 1 | `ProposedEffect` | `ProposedEffect(u64)`: the proposal's number | an effect awaiting an authority holder's decision | `tasks` |
| 2 | `Repository` | `Repository(Repository)`: forge, repository | an adopted repository: its project, role, kinds, protection and settings | `repositories` |
| 3 | `Hold` | `Hold(Name)` | a held resource, its task, its writer and its drift | `holds` |
| 4 | `Names` | `Names(u64)`: the task | the live resources one task names | `tasks` |
| 5 | `Subscription` | `Subscription { task, topic }` | one task's subscription to a topic | `subscriptions` |
| 6 | `BranchHead` | `BranchHead(Name)` | a branch's last observed tip | the client's `resources` |
| 7 | `PullState` | `PullState(Name)` | a pull request's last published state | the client's `resources` |
| 8 | `Ci` | `Ci { repository, head }` | the last CI verdict published for a commit | `subscriptions` |
| 9 | `Landed` | `Landed(Commit)` | a merge temper made, and its task | `landings` |

| 12 | `Change` | `Change(u64)`: the task | a change procedure's state | `changes` |
| 13 | `Issue` | `Issue(u64)`: the goal | a goal's issue projection | `issues` |
| 14 | `Release` | `Release(u64)`: the task | a closing task's release | `tasks` |
| 15 | `Application` | `Application(u64)`: the people child's token for the request | a keyed adoption's outcome, or a keyed outcome of the FORGE family, kept until its retention ends (jig's `domain/connectors.md`, 5.5: a connector keeps its keyed requests' outcomes durably); an outcome carrying a link is kept in memory alone, until its state's life ends (`setup.state_life_ms` after `Done`; `../client/README.md`, section 8) | `applications` |

Written by the first sign-in alone, never erased. A family of the forge connector's though it is people's, as `Demo` is: `Stored` holds every family of temper's, restored in the one restart step and written in the root's decisions, so it takes no range of its own | `bindings`, the configured members |

Written in the end's decision, erased on the act's saved answer, sent again at each start | `tasks`, plus one |

- **Loaded:** every family at the forge's restart step, and never
  otherwise. No party reads them: the engine serves none of the forge's
  client parts but the adoption form (`../client/README.md`, section 4).
- **Written** in the root's decisions, as the forge asks: its `Save` and
  `Erase` become the root's `Write::Save(Record::Forge { row })` and
  `Write::Erase(Key::Forge(key))`, which the routing encodes into jig's
  application writes, `Put { key, value }` and `Remove { key }`, in the
  same commit as jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, sections 3 and 6).
- **A new variant** of `Stored` is a new family at the next byte, and
  moves `STORE_VERSION` (section 4).

## 3. Keys

- **Under the application's tag,** 2, which jig's component prefixes and
  never lets temper's bytes leave (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.2).
  temper's own bytes start with the family's byte (section 2), then the
  key's fields.
- **By jig's rule** (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.1), written with
  skein-kv's key writer: every number as eight bytes, narrower ones
  widened; a commit as its 32 bytes, a byte string; a branch as the list
  of its segments. So:
  - `Name` is the forge, the repository, then its `What`: 0 for the
    repository, 1 and the branch's segments, 2 and a pull request's
    number, 3 and an issue's;
  - `Topic` is its variant (0 `Landings`, 1 `Ci`, 2 `Pull`, 3
    `Participation`), then the repository, then the branch, the commit
    or the number;
  - the client's key is its variant (0 `Live`, 1 `Repository`), then its
    resource or repository, a resource's `What` tagged as `Name`'s is,
    its branch one byte string.
- **Written out, not generated:** each family has a small encoder and
  decoder in `temper-protocol-store`. A property test holds each encoder
  to the order `Key` derives, on generated keys of every family, and
  each decoder to its encoder; a decoder refuses a tag it does not know,
  a number past its field's width, and trailing bytes.
- **A key and its record agree.** A row's value holds the whole record,
  its key's fields among them; the routing checks that a decoded
  record's key encodes to the row's key, and a row where they differ is
  one that does not decode, which stops the engine (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, 7.1).

## 4. Records and the store version

- **One schema,** in `temper-protocol-store`, with a record for each
  family and for every value those records carry (the client's entries,
  effects, writes, live rows and cached answers; the change's and the
  issues' states; a named call's payload, with the forge's answers and
  errors), generated by skein's generator (skein's `codec.md`). The
  domain's values are translated to and from the generated ones by small
  total functions, exhaustive both ways, so a domain change to a durable
  field breaks a translation's build.
- **Collections** are pieces or counted lists by jig's rule (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, 5.1). The schema's limits are made from the
  forge's limits, and checked against the schema's ceilings at start.
- **`STORE_VERSION`,** temper's store version: one number for all of
  temper's families. Until the first release it stays at its current
  value while its schema, generated code and one set of golden bytes
  change in place. It moves at a release, by the rule temper's versions
  share (`README.md`, section 9; skein's `codec.md`, section 5).
  The routing gives it to jig's component, whose format row records it;
  a store written with another refuses to open, saying so (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, 5.2 and 5.3).
- **`STORE_VERSION` is 10,** with the demo end's durable acts, family 19
  (below). Version 9 came with people v12 refusal bytes embedded in
  application outcomes.
- **The adoption's no-CI choice** is the appended `NoCiChoice` forge
  refusal in a retained `Application` outcome (family 15). It says no
  repository was adopted: the person must choose whether to land without
  CI or name replacement checks (`../domain/forge.md`, 8.7). Either retry
  uses a fresh application key, so the retained refusal cannot answer the
  choice again. Its tag is appended in place under the pre-release rule;
  existing tags and encoded rows keep their values and bytes. The store
  version stays 10. Golden bytes and roundtrips cover the new reason and
  preserve the previous cases.
- **The CI-setup read's answer** is the appended `CiSetup` variant of
  the stored `Answer`, with `actions: option bool` and `workflows:
  option bool`, as skein's `forge.md`, 3.3 specifies. Its translation is
  total in both directions, including unknown facts. The variant is
  appended in place under the same pre-release rule, with old tags and
  bytes preserved, new golden and roundtrip coverage, and version 10.
  Version 8 came with the `Binding` family, tag 18,
  `Key::Binding(address)` (section 2), its row `{ address, subject }`: the
  folded member address and its first Google subject (`sign-in.md`,
  section 5). The move from 7 to 8 breaks the stored format as each move
  does: a store written at 7 refuses to open, saying so.
- **`STORE_VERSION` 7 came** with a repository's tag made its forge's
  number and its id, packed (`../domain/forge.md`, section 4): the
  tasks' resource parameters and saved resources jig keeps name the
  pair, where 6's named the forge's id alone. The schema is 6's; the
  move from 6 to 7 breaks the stored format as each move does: a store
  written at 6 refuses to open, saying so, rather than resolving its
  tags to no repository later.
- **The demo's project,** its number in the `Demo` row, written by the
  demo's start once it has made the project (setup.md, 8.4;
  `../draft/first-run.md`, 2.4): designed, and waiting on jig's
  projects made at runtime. Its code takes the next `STORE_VERSION` at
  landing; none is reserved.
- **The demo's end's acts,** the `DemoAct` family, tag 19 (section
  2), its row `{ key, act }`, the act `Close { project, task }` or
  `RemoveAccount { account }`: the grace and the reason are the end's
  constants and the document's, read again at each send, so they are
  not stored. Version 10 adds family 19 after `Binding`, family 18;
  a store written at 9 refuses to open, saying so.
- **`STORE_VERSION` 6** came with the `Demo` family, tag 17,
  `Key::Demo(forge)` (section 2), its row `{ forge, state }`, the
  state running or ended: the demo's state, the engine's alone
  (setup.md, 8.4), shaped so that its end adds nothing to it. 4 and 5
  changed records in place, 5 jig's exceeded holds in the client's
  refusals. The move from 5 to 6 breaks the stored format as each move
  does: a store written at 5 refuses to open, saying so.
- **`STORE_VERSION` 3** came with the `Writer` family, tag 16,
  `Key::Writer(forge)` (section 2): each forge's writer, given at
  runtime, kept by skein's forge client as its own record (skein's
  `forge-client.md`, 7.1), and not secret (credentials.md, 2.1). The
  move from 2 to 3 breaks the stored format: a store written at 2
  refuses to open, saying so (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 5.3). Tag 15's
  outcome, which also holds the FORGE family's keyed outcomes and
  whose adoption carries the forge's kind, is that move's, in place.
  Pre-release, nothing is translated; upgrades stay open (section 7).
  Another owner's move in the same window is this one: the version
  moves once (`README.md`, section 9).
- **A deployment made again** after such a break: stop temper, move its
  store aside, the `store/` and `transcripts/` directories in the
  deployment's directory that jig's `deployment` member names
  (configuration.md, section 2; jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 2),
  and start afresh, a first start (configuration.md, section 8). What
  survives:
  - the forges' records, only where `secrets/`, jig's secrets' store, is
    kept and its own format still matches (jig's `protocol/store.md`,
    section 9; credentials.md, 2.1); otherwise each forge is connected
    again;
  - the configuration and its private files, which no store holds;
  - nothing else: jig sweeps its own secrets the new store names none
    of, so LLM accounts are signed in again (jig's `protocol/store.md`,
    section 9); owners and projects are seeded again from `engine`, and
    each repository is adopted again through the client
    (configuration.md, section 8); tasks, goals and temper's families
    stay in the store moved aside, and the deployment's id is drawn
    again, so its branch namespace is new.
- **Sizes.** `temper-protocol-store` computes, from the forge's limits,
  the largest key and the largest value of its families, and hands them
  with `STORE_VERSION` to jig's component, which checks them with its own
  against skein-kv's limits, and the start is refused when they do not
  fit (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 5.4).

## 5. The restart

- **One step, one interval.** The core's restart script asks for the
  forge connector's step (`RestoreConnector`, jig's `domain/engine.md`,
  section 6). The root then loads `Range::Forge` a page at a time,
  restores each row into the forge (its `Event::Restore`), and ends the
  step with its `Restored`.
- **The routing maps `Range::Forge`** to one interval: every key under
  temper's tag, which it asks jig's component to restore as the
  application's interval (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.2 and section
  12). The routing encodes the root's cursor with temper's key encoder,
  decodes each row with temper's codecs before the root sees it, and
  keeps the longest prefix of the page within the root's rows and
  decoded bytes, its cursor the last key kept.
- **Family by family,** in the variants' order, since the family's byte
  leads each key. The forge restores each row into its own table and
  reconciles them all at `Restored`, so it takes the rows in any order.
- **Every row the predicate admits.** `Range::contains` admits every
  forge key and no core key; a test checks the interval against it on
  generated keys of both (section 6).
- **Nothing else is read:** no load, get or party's read of temper's
  families follows the restart.
- **The core's ranges are jig's, filtered.** jig's restart ranges
  (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.3 and 7.2) are coarser than the root's
  core ranges, so the routing asks for the jig ranges that hold a root
  range and keeps each row the root's range admits: `CoreRange::Tasks`,
  the tasks child's live families with the goals' projections, which
  are core rows, is jig's `[Core, Tasks]`, in that order, which is also
  the keys' order; `CoreRange::{Calls, RunProofs}` are jig's `[Core]`.
  A root cursor is a key of the root's range, and names the jig
  interval that holds it. A page whose rows the filter all drops, while
  jig's walk goes on, is asked again from jig's cursor before the root
  hears of it, so a page that goes on keeps a row, and its cursor is the
  last key kept.
- **The census is jig's walk, taken as it comes.** `Range::Census` is
  jig's census (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.2): a page's rows are ended
  read positions and ended tasks in the walk's order, the same task once
  for each index row that names it, and its cursor is the key of the
  last index row walked, which jig's `Census::interval` places in the
  walk. temper keeps no copy of the walk's order.
- **The root checks the census's rows, and keeps none.** Each row is an
  ended task or an ended read position, and the cursor a key the census
  admits, past the page's start in the walk's order; a page may hold no
  row and go on. The root neither pairs, orders nor dedupes the rows: it
  hands each to jig's core as it comes. jig's component never parts a
  position from its task, so the root's `loads.rows` is at least 2,
  checked at start. A cut census page stops the restart, as every cut
  restart page does.

## 6. Testing

Within the suites' budgets (`../domain/testing.md`, section 2):

- **Step tests** in `temper-protocol-store`: each family's key encoder
  against the derived order, and its decoder against its encoder; the
  interval of `Range::Forge` against `Range::contains` over generated
  keys; each codec both ways, with golden bytes; each translation both
  ways, a domain value equal to itself after a round trip; the schema's
  bounds at their limit and one past; the sizes against the forge's
  limits.
- **Fuzz targets** for each family's decoder and for the key decoder.
- **The real store in the domain worlds,** as jig's `protocol/store.md`,
  section 14 has it: temper's engine worlds run jig's component over
  skein-kv's in-memory mode, with temper's families encoded by
  `temper-protocol-store`, so every story runs the real keys, codecs and
  interval, with the world's faults: commits held, delayed, one failed
  to stop the engine, and a crash keeping what reached the store.
- **The one-process world** (`tests/one-process`, `../domain/testing.md`, 2.5):
  the store on skein's crashing disk, the process killed and restarted
  at drawn moments; after each restart the forge's records are those of
  the last acknowledged commit, or of that and one whole uncertain one,
  never part of one.
- **The store's own world** is jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section
  14); temper adds none.

## 7. Open questions

- **`Landed` grows with history.** A merge temper made is kept for
  own-change news, and no row is erased; once the forge's `landings`
  limit is reached, later merges are not recorded, and news of them reads
  as another's landing. Default: bounded by the forge's limits, no
  retention. A row could go once no live goal's subscription could hear
  of its merge.
- **Upgrades:** reading an older `STORE_VERSION` as it loads, or a pass
  at opening that rewrites it, once a deployment's data must outlive a
  release. Default: jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 16), a store
  of another version refused, and the deployment made again (section 4).
- **Indexes for the forge's client parts,** once panels are served
  (`../client/README.md`, section 4): a family of small rows per view, as jig's
  indexes are (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.4). Default: none until
  they are served.
